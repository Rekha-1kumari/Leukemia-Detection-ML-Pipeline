The Four Primary Classifications of Leukemia

Differentiating acute versus chronic onset and lymphoid versus myeloid cell lineages.

ALL

Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

Rapid proliferation of immature lymphoblasts that crowd out bone marrow factories. Most common in pediatric patients with high cure rates when diagnosed early.

AML

Acute Myeloid Leukemia

Fast-progressing malignancy of myeloid precursors, more prevalent in older adults. Microscopic smears often exhibit pathognomonic crystalline Auer rods.

CLL

Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

Indolent, slow-growing accumulation of mature-appearing but incompetent B-lymphocytes. Peripheral smears typically exhibit fragile smudge cells.

CML

Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

Associated with the Philadelphia chromosome $t(9;22)$ BCR-ABL1 translocation. Successfully treated using targeted Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKI).
